Comparative stove pilot design

One traditional Chorkor kiln that processors already use, against one modified Ahotor oven bought from CSIR-FRI. Same site, same processor, same fish, same fuel, measured side by side.

What we compare

Two smoking systems, side by side

The traditional kiln is the baseline and is not purchased. The improved oven is the only equipment we buy for the pilot.

Arm A · Baseline

Traditional Chorkor kiln

Source
Existing kiln at the site, owned or rented by the processor. No purchase.
Design
Rectangular mud or brick firebox; stacked wire-mesh trays sit directly over the fire.
Size
Standard about 225 × 112 × 60 cm; 5–10 trays of about 20 kg fresh fish each.
Fuel
Fuelwood, lit with waste cardboard.
Fat control
None. Fat drips onto the fire.
Arm B · Improved · Buy 1

Modified Ahotor oven (CSIR-FRI)

Source
CSIR-Food Research Institute design, built locally by approved artisans.
Design
Enclosed combustion chamber inside a Chorkor-like shell, with grate and primary and secondary air inlets.
Fat control
Fat-collecting tray between fire and fish, dusted with ash.
Fuel
Fuelwood, charcoal or LPG variants.
Price
Quote requested. Past reports range from GHS 1,768 (2019) to about GHS 10,000 (2024).
Arm C · Optional

Retrofitted Chorkor

Source
An existing Chorkor fitted with a fat-collector tray (CSIR-FRI design, in trial since 2026).
Why
Tests whether a low-cost upgrade to a kiln women already rent gets most of the benefit.
Cost
Low; tray and fitting only. Quote to request together with Arm B.
How they work

Where the smoke and fat go

The main design difference is what happens to the fat. In the Chorkor it lands on the fire; in the Ahotor a tray catches it.

Site set-up

One shed, one processor, two kilns

Placing the new oven next to an existing Chorkor means weather, fish and the processor's skill are the same for both. Any difference comes from the stove.

Each test day

Paired batch protocol

Both kilns run the same day, in the same order of steps. We propose at least 10 paired batches; the final number is to be agreed with the supervisor.

  1. Split the fishWeigh one batch of the same species (mainly chub mackerel) and divide it equally between the two kilns.
  2. Allocate and weigh the fuelGive each kiln fuelwood from the same supplier and record its starting weight. Note the ignition material used.
  3. Start both kilns togetherRecord the start time, tray count and who is operating each kiln.
  4. Monitor during smokingLog CO and PM2.5 at breathing height, kiln temperature, tray turns and any flare-ups.
  5. Finish and weighRecord the end time, the weight of smoked fish and the weight of unused fuel and charcoal left.
  6. Sample and score the fishKeep samples for PAH testing, and ask the processor and two buyers to rate colour, texture and taste.

Before we buy

Procurement and compliance checklist

  • Research leadQuote from CSIR-FRIPrice for one modified Ahotor (and the Arm C retrofit), fuel options, test data, approved builder and build time.
  • Research leadAsk the Energy Commission about L.I. 2454Confirm whether the cookstove regulation applies to fish-smoking ovens, and what registration is needed.
  • If L.I. 2454 appliesTest at CSIR-RTKC under GS ISO 19867-1Thermal efficiency at least 25% (wood) or above 35% (charcoal), safety score 85.
  • SupervisorEthics approval and consentWritten agreement with the processor and kiln owner, covering monitoring, fish ownership and what they receive.
  • TeamAdd the oven to the budgetThe current budget covers sensors, calibration and mobile-money payments, but has no line for the oven.
  • TeamRecord the procurement routeLocally built, supplier, invoice and test reports. Carbon-credit verification will need this.
After the pilot

What the results feed into the Carbon Clarity stove

The pilot sets the design requirements for our own stove in Phase 2.

Fat controlKeep drippings off the fire, as the Ahotor tray does.
Capacity and speedMatch the Chorkor's batch size and time, the main reason women stay with it.
The smoked lookDeliver the colour and flavour buyers pay for.
Affordable to own or retrofitPriced for women who rent kilns day to day.
Sensor-readyBuilt-in points for the fuel and temperature sensors.
