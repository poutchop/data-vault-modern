Ghana's carbon market: what is happening now

Ghana is Africa's first country to authorise, issue, transfer and report Paris Agreement carbon credits. This research maps the rules, deals, projects and money, and asks who benefits, so Carbon Clarity's stove credits are built on solid ground.

91projects in Ghana's carbon pipeline
11projects authorised, about 12.7 MtCO2e
58%of the pipeline is clean cooking
34,705credits transferred to Switzerland (reported, being verified)
$21.8mREDD+ results-based payments in 2025

Why it matters

What this means for Carbon Clarity

  • Clean cooking dominates. Cookstove projects are 58% of Ghana's pipeline and supplied its first transferred credits, the same space as Carbon Clarity's fish-smoking stove.
  • Integrity is under scrutiny. Global studies report over-crediting in cookstove methods, so audit-ready field data is a real advantage.
  • Benefit sharing is the open question. How carbon revenue reaches communities and women is still unclear in Ghana, which is exactly the gap Carbon Clarity aims to close for fish smokers.
  • The rules are still being finalised. The Carbon Market Regulations, 2026 are in draft, so the project should track them closely.

Findings so far

What we know

Mainly from the Carbon Market Office's Annual Progress Report (August 2026).

Rules and institutions

  • The Environmental Protection Act, 2025 (Act 1124) names the EPA's Carbon Market Office as the national authority and creates a Carbon Market Committee and a mitigation fund.
  • The Carbon Market Regulations, 2026 are still in draft with the Attorney-General.
  • The Ghana Carbon Registry is live and has recorded authorisations, issuances and a transfer.
  • Ghana's NDC targets a 64 MtCO2e cut by 2030.

Pipeline by type

Estimated government revenue from credit sales: about $66 million by 2030.

Trends

  • From policy to real transactions: credits are being issued and transferred.
  • Regional leadership: Ghana is offering to lead an Africa-wide peer-learning programme.
  • More buyer countries: South Korea and Liechtenstein joining.
  • First formal accounting to the UN (corresponding adjustment) due December 2026.

Challenges

  • Heavy reliance on cookstove projects.
  • Weak verification: verifiers averaged 2.25 out of 5.
  • Farmers poorly understand payments; low women's participation.
  • Funding gaps and regulations not yet passed.
